# Evaluation of Water, Environmental, and Sustainable Development Courses in the Undergraduate Civil Engineering Curricula of Iranian Universities: A Comparison of the Old and New Curricula

## 📌 Overview

This study examines the integration of water, environmental engineering, and sustainable development into undergraduate Civil Engineering curricula in Iranian universities. It provides a comparative analysis of the old curriculum and the revised curriculum introduced in 2024 (1403), focusing on course content, credit allocation, and the classification of relevant subjects.

## 🔬 Research Methodology

The study employs a comparative and analytical approach based on document and curriculum content analysis. The official undergraduate Civil Engineering curricula approved before and after the 2024 revision were examined in terms of:

- Course content and relevance to water, environmental engineering, and sustainable development
- Credit allocation across compulsory, specialized elective, and elective courses
- Changes in course classification and educational priorities
- The integration of sustainability-related subjects into the revised curriculum

## 📊 Key Findings

- **Increased compulsory coverage:** The share of water-, environment-, and sustainability-related subjects within compulsory specialized courses increased from 9% in the old curriculum to 21% in the revised curriculum.
- **Expanded course offerings:** The revised curriculum introduced or incorporated additional subjects related to sustainable development, land-use planning, and waste management.
- **Broader elective opportunities:** The revised curriculum provides specialized elective courses grouped into thematic areas, including water and water systems, and environment and sustainable development.
- **Stronger sustainability integration:** The findings indicate a shift toward more systematic integration of environmental considerations and sustainable development into undergraduate Civil Engineering education.
