# Bachelor Thesis – Water, Environment, and Sustainable Development

Evaluation of Water, Environmental, and Sustainable Development Courses in Undergraduate Civil Engineering Programs at Selected Asian Universities

## 📌 Overview

This bachelor's thesis investigates how water resources, environmental protection, and sustainable development are incorporated into undergraduate Civil Engineering curricula at five selected Asian universities. The research examines the content, structure, and extent of relevant courses, comparing the educational approaches adopted by universities in different academic and national contexts.

The study focuses on the importance of preparing future civil engineers to address contemporary challenges such as water scarcity, environmental pollution, unsustainable urban development, and the growing need for environmentally responsible infrastructure.

By examining university curricula and comparing the distribution of relevant subjects, the research identifies differences in educational coverage, the balance between theoretical and practical learning, and opportunities to strengthen sustainability-related education in Civil Engineering.

The findings provide a comparative perspective on how undergraduate engineering education can better integrate water, environmental, and sustainability concepts to respond to emerging professional and societal needs.

## 🎯 Research Objectives

The primary objective was to evaluate and compare the integration of water resources, environmental protection, and sustainable development into undergraduate Civil Engineering curricula at selected Asian universities.

The study pursued the following objectives:

- Identify courses related to water, environment, and sustainable development in each university's undergraduate Civil Engineering curriculum.
- Compare the number, distribution, and structure of relevant courses across the selected universities.
- Examine differences in the depth and breadth of educational coverage across the three subject areas.
- Investigate the balance between theoretical instruction and practical learning opportunities.
- Explore potential relationships between curriculum coverage and broader indicators, including university rankings, Civil Engineering rankings, student population, and national economic growth.
- Develop recommendations for improving the integration of sustainability-related subjects into undergraduate Civil Engineering education.

## 🌏 Universities Included in the Study

The comparative analysis covered five universities in Asia:

1. **Amirkabir University of Technology (Tehran Polytechnic)** — Iran
2. **Qatar University** — Qatar
3. **University of Malaya** — Malaysia
4. **Chiang Mai University** — Thailand
5. **Indian Institute of Technology Delhi (IIT Delhi)** — India

These institutions were selected to provide a comparative perspective on curriculum structures and educational approaches in different Asian higher-education contexts.

## 🔬 Research Methodology

The study employed an applied, qualitative, and comparative research approach based on documentary analysis and curriculum content analysis.

Data were collected from official academic documents, including undergraduate study plans, course descriptions, curricula, and other relevant university sources. The collected information was then examined to identify and compare courses associated with the three main research areas.

The methodological framework included:

- **Document analysis:** Reviewing official university curricula and academic study plans.
- **Course identification:** Identifying subjects directly or indirectly related to water resources, environmental protection, and sustainable development.
- **Comparative curriculum analysis:** Examining similarities and differences in course offerings across the five universities.
- **Theoretical and practical learning assessment:** Considering the role of laboratory work, workshops, field activities, and research projects where reflected in the curricula.
- **Contextual comparison:** Exploring potential relationships between curriculum coverage and selected institutional and national indicators.
- **Synthesis and recommendations:** Using the comparative findings to identify educational gaps and suggest improvements.

The study was primarily based on existing academic documents rather than surveys or experimental data collection.

## 📊 Key Findings

### 1. Integration of Water, Environment, and Sustainability

The study found that all five universities included courses related to water resources, environmental protection, and sustainable development in their undergraduate Civil Engineering programs. However, the extent, depth, and structure of this coverage differed across institutions.

The relevant topics were incorporated through dedicated courses and, in some cases, through modules or content integrated into other technical subjects.

### 2. Differences in Curriculum Coverage

The comparison identified differences in the number and diversity of relevant courses among the selected universities. Some institutions provided broader coverage of the three subject areas, while others offered a more limited range of related subjects.

The findings suggest that the presence of sustainability-related topics in a curriculum does not necessarily mean that these subjects receive equal attention or sufficient depth.

### 3. Balance Between Theoretical and Practical Learning

The research found that theoretical and classroom-based instruction remained prominent in many of the programs examined.

At the same time, the curricula of some institutions, particularly Amirkabir University of Technology and the University of Malaya, included practical learning opportunities such as laboratory activities, workshops, fieldwork, and research projects.

The study highlighted the potential value of strengthening practical learning to help students apply theoretical knowledge to real-world water and environmental challenges.

### 4. Differences in Educational Approaches

Although the three research areas appeared across all five curricula, universities differed in how they organized and delivered the relevant content.

The comparison emphasized differences in course depth, the balance between theory and practice, and the extent to which sustainability concepts were integrated into broader Civil Engineering education.

### 5. Relationship with Institutional and National Indicators

The thesis explored possible relationships between the extent of sustainability-related curriculum coverage and indicators such as university rankings, Civil Engineering rankings, student population, and national economic growth.

The findings suggested that these relationships were complex and potentially influenced by several factors. The research also noted that broader economic and institutional conditions should be considered when interpreting such comparisons.

These findings should be understood as observations from the selected cases rather than evidence of a direct causal relationship between curriculum coverage and economic performance.

## 💡 Recommendations

Based on the comparative analysis, the thesis proposed several measures to strengthen water, environmental, and sustainability education in undergraduate Civil Engineering programs:

- **Curriculum development:** Regularly review and update course content to reflect emerging environmental challenges, technological developments, and professional requirements.
- **Broader subject coverage:** Strengthen the representation of water resources, environmental protection, and sustainable development throughout undergraduate education.
- **Practical learning:** Expand laboratory activities, fieldwork, applied projects, and other opportunities to connect theoretical concepts with real-world engineering problems.
- **Interdisciplinary learning:** Encourage connections between Civil Engineering, environmental science, water management, and sustainable development.
- **Industry engagement:** Involve professionals working on infrastructure and environmental projects in practical teaching activities and workshops.
- **Updated educational resources:** Incorporate current technical resources, relevant standards, and contemporary approaches to sustainable engineering.

The overall recommendation is to move beyond the limited inclusion of individual sustainability-related courses toward a more coherent and integrated approach to engineering education.
